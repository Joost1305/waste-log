Employees and managers only see the restaurants that are ticked for them. Organisation admins always see all restaurants of the organisation.

2. First setup, in order

Everything is under Settings. On a phone, Settings is a list; tap an item to open it. Doing it in this order saves you going back and forth.

  1. OrganisationName, currency, language and the default value per kg: the price used for waste that has no price of its own.
  2. RestaurantsOne per kitchen or outlet. Set the city (for weather), the open days (so "per day" figures ignore days you are closed) and whether the public impact page is on.
  3. Sections (optional, Settings › Leaderboard)Counters or stations inside a restaurant, for example Grill, Salad bar, Pizza & pasta. Staff pick their section when they register; the dashboard then shows waste per section.
  4. ProductsThe list staff choose from. Check the prices; see chapter 4.
  5. Suppliers and dishes (optional)Only if you want waste per supplier or per dish.
  6. Invoices (recommended)Upload recent supplier invoices so the waste is valued at real prices.
  7. Guests per day (recommended)Fill in covers per restaurant per day. Without guests you only see kilos; with guests you see grams per guest, the fairest number to compare over time.
  8. UsersInvite your team; see chapter 3.
  9. TargetsSet a reduction target per restaurant or for the whole organisation, per week, month or year. Tip: first register 2 to 4 weeks to know your starting point.
  10. Weekly e-mailThe numbers go to the addresses you enter, per restaurant. Tick the days and the time, or pick dates in a calendar.

3. Users and access

  1. Add a personSettings › Users › + Add. Fill in name, e-mail, role and language.
  2. Choose restaurantsAll restaurants are ticked by default. Untick the ones this person should not see.
  3. InviteKeep Send an invitation by email ticked. The person gets a link and chooses their own password. You never need to know it.
  • Forgot password: people can use Forgot your password? on the sign-in page, or you tap Reset link next to their name.
  • Leaving staff: untick Active or delete them. Their registrations stay. If you add the same e-mail address later, the old account comes back.
  • Students per block: invite a new group at the start of a block, deactivate the previous group at the end.

4. Products, prices and CO₂

The product list

Products are groups that make sense in a kitchen ("Bread", "Lettuce & salad leaves"), not every article number. Each product has a Dutch and English name, a category, a price per kg and a CO₂ value per kg.

  • Import: you can import products from an Excel file, for example an export from your recipe or purchasing system.
  • Missing products: when staff type a name that is not on the list, it shows up under Missing products. Add it as a new product or link it to an existing one; past registrations are linked too.
  • ≈ before a price means the price is an estimate (the typical price in that category). Replace it with your own price when you know it.

How waste gets its value

OrderSourceWhen
1Invoice priceAverage price per kg of that product on supplier invoices from the last 90 days (Settings › Invoices).
2Product priceThe price per kg on the product in Settings.
3Default valueThe organisation's default value per kg, when the product has no price.

Tap the Waste cost card on the dashboard to see exactly how the amount was calculated and which share uses real prices.

Supplier invoices

Real invoice prices make the waste cost precise. Settings › Invoices:

  1. UploadTap + Upload invoice and choose the PDF, a photo of a paper invoice, or an Excel/CSV export from the supplier's web shop. Reading takes up to a minute.
  2. Check every lineThe app suggests the product, the kilos and the amount per line. Lines marked Please check deserve a second look. Non-food lines (deposit, delivery) are unticked. Kilos are the total net weight of the line: 2 boxes of 5 kg is 10 kg; 1 litre counts as 1 kg.
  3. SaveLines with a product, kilos and an amount give a price per kg. Waste of those products from the invoice date up to 90 days later is revalued straight away, and the product price is updated (untick the option if you do not want that).

Made a mistake? Tap Edit next to the invoice; deleting an invoice gives the waste its previous price again. You can also type an invoice in by hand.

CO₂

CO₂ values come from the RIVM Database milieubelasting voedingsmiddelen (2024, CC BY 4.0), per product where a good match exists, otherwise the average of the category. Tap the CO₂ card on the dashboard for the source per product. The figures cover production up to and including distribution; cooking and waste processing are not included, so they are on the low side.

5. Reading the dashboard

Choose a restaurant (or all) and a period at the top. Every number compares with the previous period of the same length.

BlockWhat it tells you
Summary lineOne sentence at the top: how this period compares with your baseline (or the period before), the biggest cause and the best day to use as a benchmark.
Total waste, Waste cost, Per guest, CO₂The headline numbers. Tap Waste cost or CO₂ for the calculation and sources.
TargetHow you are doing against the target for its own period.
Trend and trend per guestKilos and grams per guest over time. Per guest is the one to watch: busy weeks always produce more kilos.
By category and by reasonWhat and why. Reasons point to the solution: overproduction asks for different planning than spoilage.
Per restaurant and per sectionWhere. The section table shows each counter's share of its restaurant, plus registrations without a section.
Supplier, dish, top productsThe most concrete starting points for a conversation with the team or a supplier.
Waste per dayEvery day in the period, with the same day last week (dotted), the 4-week average and your baseline. Rain days have a light blue background.
Weather and wasteWhether you throw away more on rainy, warm or cold days. Per guest when guest numbers are filled in, each day compared with a usual day of the same weekday. It appears once there are enough days, usually after 4 to 8 weeks.
Days in a rowStreaks and badges per restaurant (7, 30, 100 open days in a row) with a printable certificate.
Usage per locationWhen each restaurant and section last registered. Slipping after 4 open days without registrations, inactive after 14. The Reminder button gives a message to copy into WhatsApp, Teams or e-mail.
A day without registrations counts as no data, never as zero waste. Kilos per day, waste per guest and the comparison with the period before only use days on which something was registered. This way a kitchen that stops registering does not look like a kitchen that stopped wasting.

Use Print for a meeting or Export to Excel to work with the numbers yourself.

The numbers are as good as the registrations. Look at the number of registrations and the share of registrations with a photo before drawing conclusions.

6. Records, photos and corrections

  • Records: all registrations of your restaurants, with filters for period, restaurant, category and reason. Edit or delete single items, or tick several and delete them at once.
  • Gallery: the photos, heaviest first. Show the top photos of the week in the team briefing; it works better than any chart.
  • Spot checks: once a week, scan for odd weights (a typo of 50 kg instead of 5.0 kg) and correct them.

7. Interventions and best practices

Under Improve you write down every change you try, and the app measures the effect.

  1. Start an interventionNew intervention: what you change ("Soup in two smaller batches"), why, the restaurant, the start date and optionally a focus (only bread, only one reason).
  2. Let it runAfter 7 days the app compares the period before and after: per guest when guest counts are filled in, otherwise per open day. Other restaurants in the same weeks serve as a comparison.
  3. Check the effectTap Before / after for the result and a weekly chart. With few registrations the app warns that the percentage is only an indication.
  4. Share what worksTurn a successful intervention into a best practice, with a photo or PDF, so other kitchens can copy it.

8. The leaderboard

Optional, switched on in Settings › Leaderboard. Sections compete on good registering and good ideas, not on who throws away the least (that would only encourage hiding waste).

  • Points for: registering on open days, quality (photo, weighed), prevention ideas, ideas the admin adopts, and improvement compared with the previous four weeks.
  • Prevention question: after saving, staff are asked how it could have been prevented. You review ideas: Adopt or Not now.
  • Winner: at the end of the week or month, choose the winner; it goes into the hall of fame.
  • Kitchen screen: a large-screen version for a TV or tablet in the kitchen.
  • Switched off: no question after saving, no points and no leaderboard page. Sections stay in use for the dashboard.

9. Sharing results

  • Weekly e-mail: kilos, value, per guest, top 3 products, reasons, target and running interventions, per restaurant, to as many addresses as you like. Under When you tick the days and the time (Monday 07:00 by default), or choose Only on dates I pick and tap the dates in the calendar, for example to skip holidays. Each e-mail covers the 7 days before the send day. Use Preview to see it first.
  • Public impact page: switch it on per restaurant (Settings › Restaurants) for a page you can show to guests, for example via a QR code on the menu. It shows totals and trends, never names.

10. Data, export and audit log

  • Data export (Settings): one Excel file with every registration, guest counts, summaries, products, suppliers, dishes and targets. Use it for research, reports or your own backup.
  • Audit log (Settings): who changed what and when, including the old and the new value. Useful when a number suddenly looks different.
  • System check (Settings, platform admin only): every morning around 06:25 the app tests the website, the database, signing in, photo storage, the AI, the e-mail service, the weather and the hourly job. It also lists the important changes of the last 24 hours (prices, users, switches, sections, restaurants, targets, weekly e-mail lists) and gaps such as products without a price. Green works, orange is worth a look, red is broken. When something is red, GitHub e-mails the owner of the repository. Run now checks again straight away.

11. A weekly routine that works

Every day (2 minutes)

  • Did every section register today?
  • Remind the shift in the briefing

Every Monday (15 minutes)

  • Read the weekly e-mail or dashboard
  • Correct odd registrations
  • Pick the top product or reason of the week
  • Agree one change and start it as an intervention

12. Questions

The waste cost seems too high or too low.

Tap the Waste cost card. If a large share uses the default value, add prices to the products that are thrown away most.

Per guest shows a dash.

No guest counts are filled in for that period. Add them in Settings › Guests per day.

A section is missing on the registration screen.

Check Settings › Leaderboard › Sections for that restaurant. Sections show for every restaurant that has them, also with the leaderboard switched off.

Someone cannot sign in.

Check in Settings › Users that the person is active, then send a reset link. Invitation and reset e-mails sometimes land in spam.

Can I try things without touching real data?

Ask your WASTE log contact about a demo environment. Settings always apply to the organisation shown at the top.
